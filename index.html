I work on algorithms and lower bounds, with a particular interest in longstanding questions in fair division and communication complexity.

My work with collaborators includes bounded envy-free cake cutting and a counterexample to the direct sum conjecture. Following the earlier nonexistence result for submodular valuations, Mashbat Suzuki and I gave a counterexample to EFX that can be checked by hand.

I’m a postdoc with Haris Aziz in UNSW’s Algorithmic Decision Theory (ADT) group. Previously, I held postdocs with Serge Gaspers at UNSW, Haris and Toby Walsh at CSIRO’s Data61, and Ariel Procaccia at Carnegie Mellon. I completed my PhD at UNSW with Serge and Toby.

01Selected contributions

STOC 2025

The direct sum conjecture

Can solving several instances of a problem together save communication? Abdallah Saffidine and I showed that it can, giving a counterexample to the strongest form of the direct sum conjecture for total functions in deterministic communication complexity.

Read the paper ↗

STOC & FOCS 2016 · CACM 2020

Bounded envy-free cake cutting

Haris Aziz and I developed bounded protocols for dividing a cake so that no one prefers another person’s share. Our results for four agents and for any number of agents settled a longstanding problem and led to an invited article in Communications of the ACM.

Read the CACM article ↗

Preprint · Fair division · 2026

An EFX counterexample you can check by hand

Hannaneh Akrami, Alexander Mayorov, Kurt Mehlhorn, Shreyas Srinivas, and Christoph Weidenbach first showed that envy-freeness up to any good (EFX) can fail for submodular valuations. Following their work, Mashbat Suzuki and I gave a different counterexample with three agents and eight goods that can be understood and checked by hand.

Our paper also gives a counterexample for approximate EFX with subadditive valuations. We have since combined our paper with theirs; these links are to the original preprints.

Our original EFX preprint ↗

Preprint · Communication complexity

How hard is it to find the best protocol?

Serge Gaspers, Tao Zixu He, and I proved that computing deterministic communication complexity from a truth table is NP-complete, answering a question raised by Yao in 1979.

Current work in fair division and mechanism design

Mashbat Suzuki and I also study which fairness guarantees are possible beyond additive valuations.

  1. Preprint2026

    When One Good Is Not Enough: EF1 and Pareto Optimality Are Not Compatible for Submodular Valuations

    Simon Mackenzie, Mashbat Suzuki

    With submodular valuations, it may be impossible to achieve both envy-freeness up to one good (EF1) and Pareto optimality.

    arXiv ↗

In a preprint with Haris Aziz and Mashbat Suzuki, we address a question from 2010 about three-facility location. Our mechanism is strategyproof in expectation and gives a constant-factor approximation. See Anchoring for Truthfulness.

Other recent work includes fair division of goods, chores, and cake (EC 2026, forthcoming) and exponential-time approximate counting (ESA 2026, forthcoming).

Related work and experiments

I’m curious about how autoformalisation can help with my work. The Beyond the Library team formalised our direct-sum paper, which prompted me to experiment with LLMs and Lean on part of our NP-hardness proof. You can explore this in the interactive Lean proof inspector (source; scope and notes). Bertram, Lai, and Hsu have also verified a three-agent variant of our cake-cutting protocol.

Research and education

I’m a postdoctoral fellow at UNSW Sydney, working with Haris Aziz in the Algorithmic Decision Theory group. I completed my PhD at UNSW in 2016, supervised by Serge Gaspers and Toby Walsh. My thesis, Upper Bounds for Cake Cutting, studied algorithms for fair division. Before that, I studied computer science (MSc) and physics (BSc) at the University of Birmingham.
